# Federal Benefits, Refundable Credits, and Income-Tested Programs
Source: CRA Publications, Income Tax Act, Canada.ca
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

## GST/HST Credit
- **Refundable** quarterly tax credit to offset the GST/HST paid by low and modest-income individuals and families.
- Automatically assessed when you file your T1 return — no separate application required.
- Payment dates: quarterly (January, April, July, October).

### 2024-2025 Maximum Amounts
| Status | Annual Amount |
|--------|--------------|
| Single (no children) | $519 |
| Married/CLP (no children) | $680 |
| Per child under 19 | $179 |
| Single parent supplement | $179 |

- **Income threshold**: Reduced by 5% of adjusted family net income exceeding $44,530 (single) or $55,662 (family) in 2024.
- Based on the previous year's tax return (July-June payment cycle).

## Canada Child Benefit (CCB)
- **Tax-free** monthly payment to eligible families with children under 18.
- Income-tested based on adjusted family net income from the previous year's return.

### 2024-2025 Maximum Benefits
| Child Age | Maximum Annual Benefit |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Under 6 | $7,787 per child |
| 6 to 17 | $6,570 per child |

- **Clawback rates**:
  - Family income $36,502 – $79,087: reduced by 7% (1 child) or 13.5% (2 children) or 19% (3 children) or 23% (4+ children).
  - Family income above $79,087: additional reduction of 3.2% (1 child) or 5.7% (2 children) or 8% (3 children) or 9.5% (4+ children).
- CCB is completely eliminated at high incomes (e.g., ~$200,000+ for one child under 6).
- **CCB is NOT taxable income** — it does not need to be reported on the return.

## Canada Workers Benefit (CWB)
- **Refundable** credit for low-income working individuals and families.
- Replaces the former Working Income Tax Benefit (WITB).

### 2024 Amounts (Federal)
| Status | Maximum CWB |
|--------|------------|
| Single | $1,518 |
| Family | $2,616 |

- **Phase-in**: 27% of working income exceeding $3,000 (single) or $3,000 (family).
- **Phase-out**: Reduced by 15% of adjusted net income exceeding $23,495 (single) or $26,805 (family).
- **Disability supplement**: Additional $784 (single) or $784 (family) if eligible for the Disability Tax Credit.
- **Advance payments**: Can apply for advance CWB through CRA My Account (up to 50% of estimated CWB paid in advance quarterly).

## Canada Carbon Rebate (CCR) — formerly Climate Action Incentive Payment
- Quarterly payment to offset the federal carbon pricing for residents of provinces where the federal backstop applies.
- Applies to: Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland.
- Does NOT apply to BC (has own carbon tax) or Quebec (has own cap-and-trade system).

| Province | Adult | Child | Rural Supplement |
|----------|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Ontario | $560 | $280 | +20% |

| Saskatchewan | $752 | $376 | +20% |

- **Rural supplement**: 20% extra for residents of small and rural communities (outside census metropolitan areas).
- Automatically calculated and paid when filing your return.

## Old Age Security (OAS)
- Monthly payment to Canadians aged 65+.
- **Not income-tested for eligibility** (based on years of residency), but subject to clawback.
- 2024 maximum OAS pension: $727.67/month (65-74), $800.44/month (75+).
- **OAS clawback (recovery tax)**: OAS is reduced by 15% of net income exceeding $90,997 (2024). Fully eliminated at approximately $148,000 (65-74) or $154,000 (75+).
- **Deferral**: Can defer OAS up to age 70 for a 36% increase (0.6%/month for up to 60 months).

## Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS)
- Monthly payment for low-income OAS recipients.
- 2024 maximum: $1,072.93/month (single), $645.84/month (spouse receives OAS).
- Fully income-tested — reduced by 50 cents for each dollar of income other than OAS.
- **TFSA withdrawals do NOT count as income for GIS** — making TFSA critical for low-income seniors.
- **RRSP/RRIF withdrawals DO count as income for GIS** — can reduce or eliminate GIS.

## Disability Tax Credit (DTC)
- **Non-refundable** federal credit for individuals with severe and prolonged impairments.
- 2024 amount: $9,872 (credit value: $9,872 × 15% = $1,480.80).
- Supplement for persons under 18: additional $5,758 (credit value: $863.70).
- Eligibility: Must be certified by a medical practitioner on Form T2201.
- **Expanded criteria (2024)**: Better recognition of mental health conditions, including episodic impairments.
- Transferable to spouse or supporting family member if not needed by the individual.
- DTC eligibility opens access to other programs: RDSP, CWB disability supplement, child disability benefit.

## Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP)
- Long-term savings plan for individuals with disabilities (DTC-eligible).
- **Lifetime contribution limit**: $200,000 (no annual limit, but government grants are maximized with $1,500/year contributions).
- **Canada Disability Savings Grant (CDSG)**: Government matches up to $3,500/year (up to $70,000 lifetime).
- **Canada Disability Savings Bond (CDSB)**: Government contributes up to $1,000/year for low-income beneficiaries (up to $20,000 lifetime).
- Contributions are NOT tax-deductible; withdrawals of government grants/bonds and investment income are taxable.

## Medical Expense Tax Credit (METC)
- **Non-refundable** credit for eligible medical expenses exceeding the lesser of 3% of net income or $2,759 (2024).
- Can claim expenses for self, spouse, and dependent children under 18.
- Common eligible expenses: prescription drugs, dental care, eyeglasses, hearing aids, mobility aids, attendant care, medical travel.
- 12-month claiming period: Can choose any 12-month period ending in the tax year.
- **Refundable Medical Expense Supplement**: Additional refundable credit for low-income working individuals (25% of METC claimed, up to $1,379 in 2024).

## Charitable Donation Tax Credit
- **Non-refundable** credit with two tiers:
  - First $200 of donations: 15% federal credit.
  - Donations above $200: 29% federal credit (33% if taxable income exceeds $220,000).
- Provincial credits are additional (rates vary by province).
- Can claim donations made by either spouse on one return (usually the higher-income spouse for maximum benefit).
- Carry forward unused donations for up to 5 years.
- **Donating publicly listed securities in-kind**: No capital gains tax on the deemed disposition + full donation credit. One of the most tax-efficient giving strategies.

## Pension Income Splitting
- Individuals receiving eligible pension income can allocate up to 50% to their spouse/common-law partner for tax purposes.
- **Eligible pension income**:
  - Age 65+: RRIF withdrawals, annuity payments from RRSP, registered pension plan income.
  - Under 65: Registered pension plan income, certain annuities from a deceased spouse's plan.
  - NOT eligible: OAS, CPP/QPP, RRSP lump-sum withdrawals.
- Both spouses file Form T1032.
- Can significantly reduce combined tax when spouses are in different brackets.
- Also reduces OAS clawback exposure for the higher-income spouse.

## Canada Pension Plan (CPP) Benefits
- **CPP Retirement Pension**: Based on contributions over working years. Maximum 2024: $1,364.60/month (at age 65).
- **Early CPP (age 60-64)**: Reduced by 0.6%/month (7.2%/year) before age 65. Maximum reduction: 36%.
- **Deferred CPP (age 65-70)**: Increased by 0.7%/month (8.4%/year) after age 65. Maximum increase: 42%.
- **CPP sharing**: Spouses can share CPP retirement pensions (both must apply).
- CPP retirement pension is taxable income.

## Employment Insurance (EI) Benefits
- **Regular benefits**: Up to 55% of insurable earnings (max $668/week in 2024) for 14-45 weeks.
- **Maternity benefits**: Up to 15 weeks at 55% of earnings.
- **Parental benefits**: Standard (up to 40 weeks at 55%) or Extended (up to 69 weeks at 33%).
- EI benefits are taxable income.
- EI clawback: If net income exceeds $79,000 (2024), must repay 30% of EI benefits received (up to 30% of total benefits).
