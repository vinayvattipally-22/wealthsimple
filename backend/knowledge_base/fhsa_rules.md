# First Home Savings Account (FHSA) Rules
Source: CRA Publication RC725, Income Tax Act, 2023 Federal Budget
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

## What is an FHSA?
The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is a registered plan introduced on April 1, 2023, that allows prospective first-time home buyers in Canada to save for a down payment tax-free. It combines the best features of both RRSPs and TFSAs:
- **Contributions are tax-deductible** (like an RRSP) — reducing taxable income.
- **Withdrawals for a qualifying home purchase are tax-free** (like a TFSA).
- **Investment growth is tax-sheltered** — no tax on interest, dividends, or capital gains inside the account.

## Eligibility Requirements
- Must be a Canadian resident aged 18 or older (or age of majority in province).
- Must be a first-time home buyer: You and your spouse/common-law partner must not have owned a qualifying home that you lived in as a principal residence at any time in the year the account is opened or the preceding 4 calendar years.
- Must have a valid Social Insurance Number (SIN).
- Can only hold one FHSA at a time (but can have multiple FHSA accounts with different institutions).

## Contribution Limits
- **Annual contribution limit**: $8,000 per year.
- **Lifetime contribution limit**: $40,000.
- **Unused annual room carries forward**: Up to $8,000 of unused annual room can carry forward to the following year, meaning the maximum contribution in a single year is $16,000 (current year $8,000 + carried forward $8,000).
- Carry-forward room only accumulates from the year the FHSA is first opened.
- There is NO carry-forward from years before the account was opened.

## Key Dates
- **Available**: April 1, 2023 onwards.
- **Account lifetime**: Maximum 15 years from opening, or until December 31 of the year the holder turns 71, whichever comes first.
- **Deadline**: If not used for a home purchase, the account must be closed by the earlier of: 15 years after opening, or December 31 of the year you turn 71.

## FHSA Contribution Limits by Year
| Year Opened | Annual Limit | Carry-Forward Possible |
|------------|-------------|----------------------|
| 2023 | $8,000 | N/A (first year) |
| 2024 | $8,000 | Up to $8,000 from 2023 |
| 2025 | $8,000 | Up to $8,000 from 2024 |
| Total Lifetime | $40,000 | Cannot exceed $40,000 |

## Tax Deduction
- Contributions are deductible from income, similar to RRSP contributions.
- Unlike RRSPs, you do NOT need earned income to generate FHSA contribution room.
- Deductions can be carried forward — you can contribute now and deduct in a future higher-income year.
- FHSA deductions do NOT affect RRSP contribution room.

## Qualifying Withdrawals (Tax-Free)
To make a qualifying (tax-free) withdrawal, you must:
1. Be a first-time home buyer at the time of withdrawal.
2. Have a written agreement to buy or build a qualifying home in Canada.
3. Intend to occupy the home as your principal residence within one year of buying or building it.
4. Be a Canadian resident from the time of the withdrawal until the home is acquired.
5. Not have acquired the qualifying home more than 30 days before the withdrawal.

## Non-Qualifying Withdrawals
- Withdrawals that do not meet the qualifying criteria are **fully taxable** as income in the year received.
- Financial institutions will withhold tax at source on non-qualifying withdrawals.
- Withdrawn amounts (qualifying or not) do NOT restore contribution room.

## Transfers
- **FHSA → RRSP/RRIF**: You can transfer FHSA funds to an RRSP or RRIF tax-free at any time. This does NOT use RRSP contribution room and is not counted as an RRSP contribution. This is useful if you decide not to buy a home.
- **RRSP → FHSA**: You can transfer funds from an RRSP to an FHSA. This uses FHSA contribution room but is NOT tax-deductible (since the RRSP contribution was already deducted). The benefit is that when you eventually withdraw from the FHSA for a home purchase, it's tax-free (whereas RRSP withdrawals are taxable).
- **FHSA → FHSA**: Transfers between FHSA accounts are allowed without tax consequences.

## FHSA vs RRSP Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)
| Feature | FHSA | RRSP HBP |
|---------|------|----------|
| Tax deduction on contribution | Yes | Yes |
| Tax-free withdrawal for home | Yes | Yes (if repaid) |
| Repayment required | No | Yes (over 15 years) |
| Maximum withdrawal | $40,000 (lifetime) | $60,000 (as of 2024) |
| Can use both simultaneously | Yes | Yes |
| Investment growth on withdrawn amounts | Tax-free permanently | Must be repaid to RRSP |

**Combined Strategy**: A first-time buyer can use BOTH the FHSA ($40,000) and HBP ($60,000) for a total of $100,000 in tax-advantaged funds for a home purchase.

## Overcontribution Penalty
- Contributions exceeding the annual or lifetime limit are subject to a 1% per month penalty tax on the excess amount.
- File Form RC725 if you have an FHSA overcontribution.

## FHSA on Death
- If the FHSA holder dies, the account can be transferred to a surviving spouse or common-law partner's FHSA or RRSP tax-free.
- If no qualifying survivor, the fair market value is included in the deceased's income.

## Key Tax Planning Strategies with FHSAs
1. **Open an FHSA immediately** even if you're not sure about buying — the 15-year clock and carry-forward room only start when the account is opened.
2. **Contribute $8,000/year** to maximize the tax deduction and tax-free growth.
3. **Combine FHSA + HBP** for up to $100,000 in tax-advantaged home purchase funds.
4. **If you don't buy a home**, transfer to RRSP tax-free — you don't lose the money, and it doesn't use RRSP room.
5. **Carry forward deductions** if you're in a low bracket now but expect higher income later.
6. **Hold high-growth investments** in the FHSA to maximize the benefit of tax-free growth.
7. **Couples**: Both partners can each open an FHSA for combined room of $80,000 (+ $120,000 HBP = $200,000 total).
