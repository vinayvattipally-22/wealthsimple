# Canadian Tax Planning Strategies
Source: Multiple CRA publications, Income Tax Act provisions
Last Updated: 2024 Tax Year

## RRSP Optimization Strategies

### Maximize RRSP Deduction Value
- Contribute when in a higher marginal tax bracket (e.g., 29% or 33% federal).
- If currently in a low bracket, consider carrying forward the deduction to a future higher-income year.
- The tax savings from an RRSP contribution equals: contribution × combined marginal rate.
- Example: $10,000 RRSP contribution at 43.41% combined rate = $4,341 tax savings.

### Spousal RRSP for Income Splitting
- Contribute to a spousal RRSP to equalize retirement income between spouses.
- The contributor claims the deduction; the lower-income spouse withdraws in retirement at a lower rate.
- Respect the 3-year attribution rule: wait 3 calendar years after last spousal contribution before withdrawing.

### RRSP Meltdown Strategy
- In early retirement (before age 65), withdraw from RRSP in low-income years to reduce future mandatory RRIF minimums.
- Objective: avoid having large RRIF withdrawals push income into higher brackets or trigger OAS clawback.

## TFSA Optimization Strategies

### Asset Location Strategy
- Place high-growth assets (equities, equity ETFs) in TFSA — gains are permanently tax-free.
- Place interest-bearing assets (bonds, GICs) in RRSP — interest is taxed at the highest rate, so sheltering it in RRSP provides the most benefit.
- Place Canadian dividend-paying stocks in non-registered accounts — eligible dividends receive the dividend tax credit.

### TFSA for Emergency Fund
- Use TFSA as an emergency fund — withdrawals are tax-free and don't affect benefit eligibility.
- Contribution room is restored the following January.

### TFSA for Retirees
- TFSA withdrawals do not affect OAS clawback threshold ($90,997 in 2024) or GIS eligibility.
- Prioritize TFSA withdrawals over RRIF/RRSP withdrawals to preserve government benefits.

## Capital Gains Strategies

### Capital Gains Inclusion Rate
- For 2024 and beyond: 50% inclusion rate on the first $250,000 of capital gains per year, 66.67% on amounts above $250,000 (for individuals).
- Capital gains are more tax-efficient than interest or employment income.

### Tax-Loss Harvesting
- Sell investments with unrealized losses to offset capital gains realized in the same year.
- Net capital losses can be carried back 3 years or carried forward indefinitely to offset future capital gains.
- Superficial loss rule: Cannot repurchase the same or identical property within 30 days before or after the sale (61-day window). If violated, the loss is denied and added to the cost base of the repurchased property.

### Capital Gains Reserve
- If selling property and receiving payment over multiple years, you can claim a capital gains reserve.
- Spread the gain over up to 5 years (10 years for qualifying farm/fishing property or small business shares transferred to a child).

## Income Splitting Strategies

### Pension Income Splitting
- Individuals receiving qualifying pension income can allocate up to 50% to their spouse/common-law partner for tax purposes.
- Qualifying pension income includes: RRIF payments (age 65+), life annuity payments from a pension plan, certain other pension payments.
- Both spouses must agree by filing Form T1032 (Joint Election to Split Pension Income).

### Prescribed Rate Loan
- Lend money to a lower-income spouse at the CRA prescribed rate (currently 4% for Q1 2024).
- The lower-income spouse invests the borrowed funds; investment income is taxed in their hands at their lower rate.
- The lending spouse must report the interest received. The borrowing spouse can deduct the interest paid.
- The loan must be a bona fide loan with interest paid annually by January 30 of the following year.

### Canada Child Benefit (CCB) Optimization
- CCB is based on adjusted family net income. RRSP contributions reduce net income and may increase CCB payments.
- For a family with $80,000 combined income and two children, a $10,000 RRSP contribution could increase CCB by approximately $400-500/year.

## Loss Carryforward and Carryback Rules

### Non-Capital Losses (Business/Employment)
- Can be carried back 3 years or forward 20 years.
- Applied against all sources of income.

### Net Capital Losses
- Can be carried back 3 years or forward indefinitely.
- Can only offset capital gains (not other income), except in the year of death when they can offset all income.

### Allowable Business Investment Losses (ABIL)
- 50% of a business investment loss on shares or debt of a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC).
- Can be deducted against all sources of income.
- Unused ABILs become non-capital losses after 10 years, then net capital losses.

## Charitable Donation Strategies

### First-Time Donor Super Credit (Expired)
- This credit expired after 2017 and is no longer available.

### Donation of Securities
- Donating publicly listed securities directly to a registered charity eliminates the capital gains tax on the donated securities entirely (0% inclusion rate).
- The donor receives a charitable donation tax credit based on the fair market value of the securities at the time of donation.
- This is one of the most tax-efficient giving strategies available.

### Carry Forward
- Unused donation credits can be carried forward up to 5 years.
- Strategy: Accumulate donations and claim them in a high-income year to maximize the 29%/33% federal credit rate.

## Employment Deductions

### Work from Home Expenses
- Employees who worked from home due to employer requirement can deduct home office expenses.
- Detailed method (Form T2200): Deduct proportionate share of rent, utilities, internet, office supplies, maintenance.
- Cannot deduct mortgage interest, property taxes, or home insurance (unless commission employee).

### Vehicle Expenses
- Employees required to use their personal vehicle for work (Form T2200 required) can deduct fuel, maintenance, insurance, CCA on the vehicle.
- Keep a detailed mileage log showing business vs. personal kilometers.
- CCA rate: 30% declining balance (Class 10) or 100% for zero-emission vehicles (Class 54).

## Medical Expenses
- Claim medical expenses for any 12-month period ending in the tax year.
- Strategy: Choose the 12-month period that maximizes the claim.
- Only expenses exceeding the lesser of 3% of net income or $2,759 (2024) generate a credit.
- The lower-income spouse should claim medical expenses (lower 3% threshold means more of the expenses qualify).
