# CPP and EI Contribution Rates and Thresholds
Source: CRA Publication T4127 — Payroll Deductions Tables, CRA CPP/EI Rate Sheets
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

## Canada Pension Plan (CPP) — Employee Contributions

### How CPP Contributions Are Calculated
CPP contributions are calculated on pensionable earnings between the basic exemption and the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE):
- Formula: (min(pensionable_earnings, YMPE) - basic_exemption) × contribution_rate
- The basic exemption is $3,500 per year (prorated for partial-year employment).
- Both the employee and employer contribute at the same rate.

|------|------|----------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| 2020 | $58,700 | $3,500 | 5.25% | $2,898.00 |
| 2021 | $61,600 | $3,500 | 5.45% | $3,166.45 |
| 2022 | $64,900 | $3,500 | 5.70% | $3,499.80 |
| 2023 | $66,600 | $3,500 | 5.95% | $3,754.45 |
| 2024 | $68,500 | $3,500 | 5.95% | $3,867.50 |
| 2025 | $71,300 | $3,500 | 5.95% | $4,034.10 |

### CPP2 (Enhanced CPP — Second Ceiling)
Starting in 2024, a second earnings ceiling (YAMPE) was introduced for enhanced CPP:
| Year | YAMPE | CPP2 Rate (Employee) | Max CPP2 Contribution |
|------|-------|---------------------|----------------------|
| 2024 | $73,200 | 4.00% | $188.00 |
| 2025 | $81,200 | 4.00% | $396.00 |

CPP2 applies to earnings between YMPE and YAMPE: (min(earnings, YAMPE) - YMPE) × 4%

### CPP Exemptions
- Employees under 18 or over 70 do not contribute to CPP.
- Employees aged 65-70 can elect to stop contributing by filing Form CPT30.
- Self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer portions (double the rate).

### Quebec Pension Plan (QPP)
Quebec residents contribute to QPP instead of CPP. QPP rates are slightly higher:
| Year | QPP Rate (Employee) | Max Employee Contribution |
|------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| 2024 | 6.40% | $4,160.00 |
| 2025 | 6.40% | $4,339.20 |

## Employment Insurance (EI) — Employee Premiums

### How EI Premiums Are Calculated
EI premiums are calculated on insurable earnings up to the maximum insurable earnings:
- Formula: min(insurable_earnings, max_insurable_earnings) × premium_rate
- There is NO basic exemption for EI.
- The employer rate is 1.4 times the employee rate (unless a reduced rate is approved).

### EI Premium Rate Table
| Year | Max Insurable Earnings | Employee Rate | Max Employee Premium |
|------|----------------------|---------------|---------------------|

| 2024 | $63,200 | 1.66% | $1,049.12 |
| 2025 | $65,700 | 1.64% | $1,077.48 |

### Quebec EI (QPIP)
Quebec residents pay a reduced EI rate because Quebec has its own parental insurance plan (QPIP):
| Year | Quebec EI Rate | QPIP Employee Rate |
|------|---------------|-------------------|
| 2024 | 1.32% | 0.494% |
| 2025 | 1.31% | 0.494% |

## T4 Box References
- **Box 16**: Employee's CPP contributions
- **Box 17**: Employee's QPP contributions (Quebec)
- **Box 18**: Employee's EI premiums
- **Box 24**: EI insurable earnings (capped at max insurable earnings)
- **Box 26**: CPP/QPP pensionable earnings (capped at YMPE)
- **Box 14**: Employment income (total, not capped)

## Key Relationships
- If Box 14 (employment income) is less than the max insurable earnings, then Box 24 = Box 14.
- If Box 14 is less than the YMPE, then Box 26 = Box 14.
- If Box 14 exceeds these caps, Box 24 and Box 26 are capped at the respective maximums.
- CPP and EI contributions generate non-refundable tax credits at the lowest federal tax rate (15%), reducing federal tax payable.
- The CPP/EI credit is calculated as: (CPP contributions + EI premiums) × 15%.
