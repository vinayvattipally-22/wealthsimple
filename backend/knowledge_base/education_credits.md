# T2202 Tuition and Education Credits — Canadian Tax Rules
Source: CRA Guide P105, T2202 Information
Last Updated: 2024

## Overview
The T2202 slip (Tuition and Enrolment Certificate) is issued by designated educational institutions in Canada. Students can claim tuition amounts on their tax return for a non-refundable tax credit.

## Tuition Tax Credit
- Federal credit rate: 15% of eligible tuition fees
- Must be from a qualifying educational institution
- Minimum tuition per institution: $100 for the year
- Includes mandatory ancillary fees (but not student union or health plan fees)

## Eligible Tuition Fees
- Tuition paid to Canadian universities and colleges
- Fees for occupational skills courses at certified institutions
- Fees paid to foreign universities (if enrolled in a course lasting at least 3 consecutive weeks)
- Professional examination fees (CPA, bar admission, medical licensing)

## Transfer Rules
- Students can transfer up to $5,000 of unused tuition credits to:
  - Spouse or common-law partner
  - Parent or grandparent
  - Spouse's/partner's parent or grandparent
- The student must first use the credit to reduce their own tax to zero
- Maximum transfer: $5,000 minus the amount used by the student

## Carryforward
- Unused tuition amounts carry forward indefinitely
- Must be claimed in the first year there is tax payable
- Cannot be transferred if the student has carryforward amounts — carryforward must be used first
- Tracked on Schedule 11 of the T1 return

## Key Tax Planning
- Low-income students should transfer credits to higher-income family members
- Carryforward is valuable for students who will have higher income in future years
- Tuition credits cannot create a refund (non-refundable credit only)
- Some provinces have eliminated provincial tuition credits — check provincial rules

## Provincial Variations
- Ontario: Eliminated the provincial tuition tax credit for 2017 and later years
- Federal tuition credit remains available across all provinces
- Some provinces still offer provincial tuition credits (e.g., BC, Alberta)
