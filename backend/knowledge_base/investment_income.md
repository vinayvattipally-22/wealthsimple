# T5 Statement of Investment Income — Canadian Tax Rules
Source: CRA Guide T4015, RC4169
Last Updated: 2024

## Overview
The T5 slip reports investment income from Canadian sources including interest, dividends, capital gains dividends, and royalties. Financial institutions must issue T5 slips when investment income exceeds $50 in a calendar year.

## Key Boxes
- **Box 13**: Interest from Canadian sources — fully taxable at marginal rate
- **Box 18**: Capital gains dividends — only 50% is taxable (inclusion rate)
- **Box 24**: Eligible dividends (actual amount)
- **Box 25**: Eligible dividends taxable amount — grossed up by 38% (multiply by 1.38)
- **Box 26**: Other than eligible dividends (actual amount)
- **Box 10/11**: Eligible dividend amounts
- **Box 12**: Actual amount of other dividends

## Dividend Taxation
### Eligible Dividends (from public corporations)
- Gross-up: 38% for 2024 (Box 25 = Box 24 x 1.38)
- Federal dividend tax credit: 15.0198% of taxable amount
- Net tax on eligible dividends is lower than on interest income

### Other Than Eligible Dividends (from CCPCs)
- Gross-up: 15% for 2024
- Federal dividend tax credit: 9.0301% of taxable amount
- Higher effective tax rate than eligible dividends

## Interest Income Taxation
- Fully included in income at marginal tax rate
- No preferential treatment (unlike dividends or capital gains)
- Includes interest from bank accounts, GICs, bonds, and T-bills
- Must be reported on an accrual basis for investments held over multiple years

## Capital Gains Dividends (Box 18)
- Received from mutual fund corporations
- Only 50% included in income (capital gains inclusion rate for 2024)
- Report on Schedule 3 of T1 return

## Tax Planning Considerations
- Hold interest-bearing investments in registered accounts (RRSP/TFSA) where possible
- Canadian eligible dividends are tax-efficient in non-registered accounts
- Capital gains dividends benefit from 50% inclusion rate
- Consider dividend tax credit when choosing between interest vs dividend investments
