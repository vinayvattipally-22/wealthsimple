# Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) Rules
Source: CRA Publication T4040 — RRSPs and Other Registered Plans for Retirement
Last Updated: 2024 Tax Year

## What is an RRSP?
A Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) is a savings plan registered with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) under the Income Tax Act. Contributions to an RRSP are tax-deductible, reducing the contributor's taxable income for the year. Investment income earned inside the plan grows tax-deferred until withdrawal.

## RRSP Contribution Limits
The RRSP deduction limit (also called "RRSP room") for a given year is calculated as:
- 18% of the previous year's earned income, up to the annual maximum
- Plus any unused contribution room carried forward from previous years
- Minus any pension adjustment (PA) from an employer pension plan

## RRSP Deduction Rules
- Contributions made in the first 60 days of a calendar year can be deducted in either the current or the previous tax year.
- The contribution deadline for a tax year is 60 days after the end of that year (typically March 1, or February 29 in a leap year).
- You do not have to deduct all contributions in the year they are made. Unused deductions can be carried forward indefinitely.
- You cannot contribute to your own RRSP after December 31 of the year you turn 71. At that point, the RRSP must be converted to a Registered Retirement Income Fund (RRIF) or an annuity.

## Overcontribution Penalty
- A lifetime overcontribution cushion of $2,000 is allowed without penalty.
- Contributions exceeding the deduction limit by more than $2,000 are subject to a penalty tax of 1% per month on the excess amount.
- The penalty applies on the last day of each month the excess exists.
- Form T1-OVP (Individual Tax Return for RRSP, PRPP and SPP Excess Contributions) must be filed.

## Spousal RRSP
- You can contribute to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP, using your own contribution room.
- The contributor gets the tax deduction; the annuitant (spouse) owns the plan.
- Attribution rule: If the annuitant withdraws within 3 calendar years of the last spousal contribution, the withdrawal is attributed back to the contributor as income.
- After the 3-year attribution period, withdrawals are taxed in the annuitant's hands.
- Spousal RRSPs are a tax planning strategy for income splitting in retirement.

## Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)
- Allows a first-time home buyer to withdraw up to $35,000 (increased to $60,000 for withdrawals made after April 16, 2024) from their RRSP tax-free to buy or build a qualifying home.
- Both spouses can each withdraw up to the maximum if both qualify.
- Repayment period: The withdrawn amount must be repaid to the RRSP over 15 years, starting the second year after the year of withdrawal.
- Minimum annual repayment: 1/15th of the total withdrawal per year.
- If the minimum repayment is not made in a year, the shortfall is added to the individual's taxable income for that year.
- To qualify: Must be a first-time home buyer (did not own a home in the 4 preceding years), and the home must be in Canada.

## Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP)
- Allows withdrawal of up to $10,000 per year (maximum $20,000 total) from RRSP for full-time education or training.
- The participant or their spouse/common-law partner must be enrolled in a qualifying educational program.
- Repayment over 10 years, starting the fifth year after the first withdrawal (or earlier if the individual ceases to be a qualifying student).
- Annual repayment: 1/10th of the outstanding balance.

## Types of Qualifying RRSP Investments
- Cash and savings deposits
- Guaranteed Investment Certificates (GICs)
- Government and corporate bonds
- Mutual funds
- Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)
- Publicly traded stocks on a designated stock exchange
- Certain mortgages and mortgage-backed securities
- Canadian-listed REITs

## RRSP Withdrawals
- Withdrawals (other than HBP/LLP) are fully taxable as income in the year received.
- The financial institution withholds tax at source:
  - 10% on amounts up to $5,000
  - 20% on amounts between $5,001 and $15,000
  - 30% on amounts over $15,000
  - In Quebec, the withholding rates are different (5%/10%/15% federal + provincial)
- Withdrawn amounts do NOT restore contribution room.

## RRSP at Death
- On death, the fair market value of the RRSP is included in the deceased's income for the year of death, unless rolled over to a qualifying survivor.
- Qualifying rollover recipients: spouse or common-law partner, financially dependent child or grandchild under 18, or financially dependent infirm child or grandchild of any age.

## Key Tax Planning Strategies with RRSPs
1. Contribute when in a higher tax bracket to maximize the deduction value.
2. Use spousal RRSPs for income splitting in retirement.
3. Consider the HBP for first-time home purchases — effectively an interest-free loan from yourself.
4. Carry forward deductions if you expect to be in a higher bracket in a future year.
5. Maximize contributions early in the year to benefit from a full year of tax-deferred growth.
6. Consider the tax impact of withdrawals in retirement — plan to withdraw in low-income years.
