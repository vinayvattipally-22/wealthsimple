# Canadian Tax Policy Changes: 2021–2025
Source: Federal Budgets, Fall Economic Statements, CRA Announcements
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

This document summarizes significant Canadian tax policy changes from 2021 to 2025, organized chronologically.

---

## 2021 Tax Year Changes

### Federal Changes
- **Basic Personal Amount**: Increased to $13,808 (ongoing enhanced BPA phase-in).
- **COVID-19 Benefits Repayment**: CERB, CRB, CRSB, CRCB payments were taxable income. Many Canadians faced unexpected tax bills when filing 2021 returns.
- **Home Office Deduction (Temporary Flat Rate)**: Extended for 2021 — employees working from home due to COVID could claim $2/day up to $500 (no T2200 required).
- **Northern Residents Deduction**: Travel benefit component temporarily suspended due to COVID travel restrictions.
- **Canada Recovery Benefit (CRB)**: Reduced from $500/week to $300/week, then ended October 23, 2021.
- **OAS Increase for 75+**: One-time $500 payment in August 2021 for seniors 75+, with permanent 10% increase starting July 2022.

### Provincial Changes
- **Alberta**: Began indexing tax brackets again after freezing indexation in 2020 and 2021.
- **New Brunswick**: Announced bracket reform effective 2022 (reducing rates and simplifying from 5 to 4 brackets).

## 2022 Tax Year Changes

### Federal Changes
- **Basic Personal Amount**: Increased to $14,398.
- **Home Office Deduction**: Temporary flat rate method ended after 2022 tax year. For 2023+, employees must use the detailed method (Form T2200 required from employer).
- **RRSP Dollar Limit**: Increased to $29,210.
- **Multigenerational Home Renovation Tax Credit (MHRTC)**: Introduced for 2023 tax year. Refundable credit of 15% on up to $50,000 of eligible renovation costs to create a secondary dwelling unit for a qualifying family member.
- **Residential Property Flipping Rule**: Announced in Budget 2022, effective January 1, 2023. Profits from selling a property held less than 365 days are automatically treated as business income (100% taxable) rather than capital gains (50% taxable), unless specific exemptions apply (death, disability, separation, job relocation, etc.).
- **Anti-Flipping Tax**: Applies to properties sold within 12 months of acquisition from January 1, 2023.
- **First Home Savings Account (FHSA)**: Announced in Budget 2022, launched April 1, 2023.
- **Underused Housing Tax (UHT)**: 1% annual tax on the value of vacant or underused residential property owned by non-resident non-Canadians. First filing required for 2022 calendar year by April 30, 2023.

### Provincial Changes
- **New Brunswick**: Reformed tax brackets — reduced from 5 brackets to 4, with significantly lower rates (e.g., lowest rate from 9.68% to 9.40%).
- **Newfoundland**: Added three new top brackets (19.80%, 20.80%, 21.30%) for high earners, making NL one of the highest-taxed provinces.
- **Nova Scotia**: Announced plans to raise the BPA (implemented gradually).

---

## 2023 Tax Year Changes

### Federal Changes
- **Basic Personal Amount**: Increased to $15,000 (completing the enhanced BPA phase-in).
- **FHSA Launched**: April 1, 2023. $8,000 annual contribution limit, $40,000 lifetime limit. Tax-deductible contributions, tax-free qualifying withdrawals.
- **Anti-Flipping Rule**: Effective January 1, 2023. Properties held less than 365 days are deemed business income unless exemptions apply.
- **Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) Reform**: Announced in Budget 2023, effective January 1, 2024. AMT rate increased from 15% to 20.5%, exemption raised from $40,000 to $173,000, broadened base including more capital gains and stock option deductions.
- **Grocery Rebate**: One-time GST/HST credit supplement paid in July 2023 to eligible low-income individuals. Up to $234 per adult, $117 per child.
- **Residential Property Flipping Rule**: First full year in effect.
- **Multigenerational Home Renovation Tax Credit**: First year claimable (for renovations completed in 2023).
- **RRSP Dollar Limit**: Increased to $30,780.
- **Employee Stock Option Deduction**: $200,000 annual vesting limit for large/established employers (introduced in 2021, fully in effect).

### Provincial Changes
- **Quebec**: Reduced lowest two tax bracket rates from 15%/20% to 14%/19% — significant tax cut for all Quebec residents.
- **Manitoba**: Began significant bracket threshold increases (continued into 2024).
- **British Columbia**: Introduced BC Renter's Tax Credit — up to $400/year for eligible renters.
- **Prince Edward Island**: Continued annual BPA increases ($750/year schedule).

## 2024 Tax Year Changes

### Federal Changes
- **Basic Personal Amount**: Increased to $15,705.
- **Capital Gains Inclusion Rate Change** (Budget 2024):
  - Effective June 25, 2024: The capital gains inclusion rate increases from 50% (one-half) to 66.67% (two-thirds) for:
    - All capital gains realized by corporations and trusts.
    - Capital gains realized by individuals exceeding $250,000 per year.
  - The first $250,000 of capital gains per year for individuals remains at the 50% inclusion rate.
  - This is the most significant capital gains tax change in decades.
  - Applies to dispositions on or after June 25, 2024.
  - Tax planning implications: Accelerate capital gains realization before June 25, 2024 if possible.
- **CPP2 (Enhanced CPP)**: Introduced second earnings ceiling (YAMPE) at $73,200. Additional 4% employee contribution on earnings between YMPE ($68,500) and YAMPE ($73,200). Maximum CPP2 contribution: $188.
- **Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)**: Withdrawal limit increased from $35,000 to $60,000 (for withdrawals after April 16, 2024).
- **HBP Repayment Grace Period**: Extended by 3 additional years for participants who made first withdrawals between 2022 and 2025.
- **Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) Reform**: New rules effective January 1, 2024. Higher AMT rate (20.5%), higher exemption ($173,000), broader base.
- **Disability Tax Credit**: Expanded eligibility criteria for mental health conditions.
- **Canadian Dental Care Plan**: Began rolling out for eligible low-income residents (not a tax credit, but income-tested benefit).
- **RRSP Dollar Limit**: Increased to $31,560.
- **Carbon Rebate Rural Supplement**: Increased to 20% (from 10%) for rural and small community residents.

### Provincial Changes
- **Manitoba**: Dramatically increased BPA from $10,855 to $15,780 and raised bracket thresholds to $47,000/$100,000 — the largest provincial tax reform in 2024.
- **Ontario**: Indexed brackets as usual, no major structural changes.
- **British Columbia**: BC Renter's Tax Credit in full effect for the first time on 2023 returns filed in 2024.
- **Alberta**: Announced indexation of brackets — thresholds increased to $148,269/$177,922/$237,230/$355,845.

## 2025 Tax Year Changes

### Federal Changes
- **Basic Personal Amount**: Increased to $16,129.
- **RRSP Dollar Limit**: Increased to $32,490.
- **TFSA Annual Limit**: $7,000 (same as 2024).
- **FHSA**: Second full year with carry-forward room available (up to $16,000 contribution in a single year if $8,000 unused from prior year).
- **CPP2 (Enhanced CPP)**: YAMPE increased to $81,200. Maximum CPP2 contribution: $396 (up from $188 in 2024). Second year of CPP2, with larger contribution amounts.
- **Capital Gains Inclusion Rate**: Full year at the new 66.67% rate for amounts over $250,000 (first full calendar year after June 25, 2024 change).
- **AMT**: Full year under new AMT rules (20.5% rate, $173,000 exemption).
- **Canada Carbon Rebate**: Amounts adjusted for inflation. Alberta and Saskatchewan residents continue to receive the highest rebates.
- **Canada Dental Care Plan**: Expected full rollout to all eligible income groups.
- **Automatic Tax Filing**: CRA expanding SimpleFile by Phone program to more eligible filers.
- **EI Premium Rate**: Decreased to 1.64% (from 1.66% in 2024).

### Provincial Changes
- **Ontario**: BPA increased to $12,399. Brackets indexed (first bracket up to $52,886).
- **Manitoba**: Continued reforms — BPA to $16,218, brackets adjusted.
- **British Columbia**: BPA to $12,932. Renter's tax credit fully established.
- **All provinces**: General indexation of brackets and credits for inflation.

## Summary of Major Multi-Year Trends (2021–2025)
1. **BPA Phase-In**: Federal BPA rose from $13,808 (2021) to $16,129 (2025) — a 16.8% increase.
2. **FHSA Introduction**: New registered account type (2023) for first-time home buyers — up to $40,000 tax-deductible.
3. **Capital Gains Reform**: Inclusion rate increased from 50% to 66.67% for gains over $250,000 (June 2024).
4. **CPP Enhancement**: CPP2 second ceiling introduced (2024), expanding mandatory contributions.
5. **COVID-19 Wind-Down**: Home office flat rate ended (2022), benefit repayments completed, return to normal rules.
6. **Anti-Flipping**: New rule deems property sales within 365 days as business income (2023).
7. **AMT Modernization**: Higher rate and broader base (2024).
8. **HBP Increase**: From $35,000 to $60,000 (2024).
9. **Provincial Reforms**: Manitoba's dramatic 2024 reform, NB rate cuts (2022), QC rate cuts (2023), NL new top brackets (2022).
10. **Inflation Indexation**: All federal and most provincial thresholds indexed annually, resulting in significant bracket creep protection.
