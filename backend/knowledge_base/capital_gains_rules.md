# Capital Gains Taxation in Canada
Source: CRA Schedule 3, Income Tax Act sections 38-55, Budget 2024
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

## What is a Capital Gain?
A capital gain occurs when you sell (dispose of) a capital property for more than its adjusted cost base (ACB) plus any outlays and expenses related to the disposition. Capital property includes stocks, bonds, real estate (other than principal residence), mutual funds, ETFs, and other investments.

## Capital Gains Inclusion Rate

### Before June 25, 2024
- **50% inclusion rate**: Only half of the capital gain is included in taxable income.
- Example: A $100,000 capital gain results in $50,000 of taxable income.

### After June 25, 2024 (Current Rules)
- **For individuals**:
  - First $250,000 of net capital gains per year: **50% inclusion rate** (unchanged).
  - Capital gains exceeding $250,000 per year: **66.67% inclusion rate** (two-thirds).
- **For corporations and trusts**:
  - **All** capital gains: **66.67% inclusion rate** (no $250,000 threshold).
- The $250,000 threshold is an annual limit — it resets each calendar year.
- It applies to NET capital gains (after applying any capital losses).

### Inclusion Rate History
| Period | Individual Rate | Corporate/Trust Rate |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| 2001 – June 24, 2024 | 50% | 50% |
| June 25, 2024 onwards | 50% (first $250K) / 66.67% (excess) | 66.67% (all) |

## Calculating Capital Gains Tax
1. **Proceeds of disposition** (selling price) − **Adjusted cost base** (purchase price) − **Outlays and expenses** (commissions, legal fees) = **Capital gain/loss**.
2. Apply the inclusion rate to get the **taxable capital gain**.
3. The taxable capital gain is added to other income and taxed at your marginal rate.

### Example (2025, Ontario resident earning $100,000)
- Sells shares for $400,000 capital gain.
- First $250,000 × 50% = $125,000 taxable.
- Remaining $150,000 × 66.67% = $100,005 taxable.
- Total taxable capital gain: $225,005.
- Combined marginal rate on top portion: ~46.41% (federal 29% + Ontario ~17.41%).
- Approximate tax on the gain: ~$104,000.

## Capital Losses
- Capital losses can only be applied against capital gains (not other income, except in year of death).
- Net capital losses can be **carried back 3 years** or **carried forward indefinitely**.
- The inclusion rate for losses matches the rate in effect when the loss was realized.
- Superficial loss rule: If you sell a security at a loss and repurchase the same or identical security within 30 days (before or after), the loss is denied and added to the ACB of the repurchased security.

## Principal Residence Exemption (PRE)
- Capital gains on the sale of your principal residence are **exempt from tax**.
- You can designate one property per family unit as a principal residence per year.
- Formula: Exempt portion = gain × (1 + years designated) / years owned.
- Reporting: Since 2016, you must report the sale of a principal residence on Schedule 3, even if fully exempt.

## Adjusted Cost Base (ACB) Rules
- For identical properties (e.g., multiple purchases of the same stock): ACB is calculated using the **average cost method**.
- Example: Buy 100 shares at $10, then 100 shares at $15 → ACB per share = ($1,000 + $1,500) / 200 = $12.50.
- Return of capital distributions reduce ACB but are not immediately taxable.
- Stock splits and consolidations adjust the per-share ACB accordingly.

## Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE)
- **Qualified small business corporation shares**: Up to $1,016,836 (2024, indexed annually) in capital gains are exempt.
- **Qualified farm/fishing property**: Same $1,016,836 exemption.
- **Canadian Entrepreneurs' Incentive** (Budget 2024): Additional $2 million lifetime exemption on eligible small business shares, phased in at $200,000/year starting 2025, with a reduced inclusion rate of one-third (33.33%). Full $2M available by 2034.
- The LCGE is a lifetime cumulative amount — once used, it reduces available room for future dispositions.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting Strategy
1. Sell investments with unrealized losses before December 31 to crystallize the loss.
2. Apply the loss against capital gains realized in the same year.
3. If no gains in the current year, carry the loss back 3 years or forward indefinitely.
4. Wait at least 31 days before repurchasing the same security to avoid the superficial loss rule.
5. Can harvest up to $250,000 in losses to offset the 50% inclusion rate gains before the 66.67% rate kicks in.

## Deemed Disposition Events
- **Emigrating from Canada**: Deemed to dispose of all capital property at fair market value (departure tax).
- **Death**: Deemed disposition at FMV, unless rolled over to spouse/common-law partner.
- **Change in use** (e.g., converting principal residence to rental): Deemed disposition at FMV.
- **Gifting property**: Deemed to dispose at FMV (cannot create artificial losses by gifting).

## Capital Gains Reserves
- If you don't receive full payment in the year of sale, you can claim a reserve for the unpaid portion.
- Maximum reserve: spread the gain over up to 5 years (10 years for qualifying farm/fishing property or shares of a family farm corporation).
- Minimum 20% of the gain must be included each year (10% for qualifying property).

## Reporting Capital Gains
- Report on **Schedule 3** of the T1 return.
- T5008 slips report securities dispositions (but may not include ACB — you must track your own ACB).
- T3 slips report capital gains from mutual funds and trusts.
- Real estate dispositions require reporting even if exempt under the PRE.

## Key Tax Planning Considerations
1. **Time gains strategically**: If you have control over timing, realize gains in years when your income (and marginal rate) is lower.
2. **Stay under $250,000**: If possible, spread capital gains across years to stay within the $250K threshold for the 50% inclusion rate.
3. **Use registered accounts**: Hold investments with the highest expected capital gains inside TFSA (permanently tax-free) or RRSP (tax-deferred).
4. **Donate securities in-kind**: Donating publicly listed securities directly to a charity eliminates the capital gains tax entirely (no taxable capital gain) and provides a charitable donation tax credit.
5. **Tax-loss harvest annually**: Review your portfolio before year-end to crystallize losses that offset gains.
6. **Track ACB meticulously**: Keep records of all purchase prices, reinvested distributions, and return of capital adjustments.
