Source: CRA T1 General Income Tax and Benefit Return, Income Tax Act sections 117, 118
Last Updated: 2025 Tax Year

## Federal Tax Brackets
Federal income tax is calculated using a progressive bracket system. Each bracket rate applies only to the portion of taxable income within that bracket.

## Basic Personal Amount (BPA)
The Basic Personal Amount is a non-refundable tax credit that every individual can claim. It reduces federal tax by BPA × 15%.

| Year | BPA |
|------|-----|
| 2020 | $13,229 |
| 2021 | $13,808 |
| 2022 | $14,398 |
| 2023 | $15,000 |
| 2024 | $15,705 |
| 2025 | $16,129 |

Note: For high-income earners (taxable income above the fourth bracket threshold), the enhanced BPA is clawed back and reduced to the base amount. The base BPA for 2024 is $14,156.

## Common Deductions from Income
Deductions reduce taxable income before tax is calculated:
- RRSP contributions (up to deduction limit)
- Union/professional dues (Box 44 on T4)
- Child care expenses
- Moving expenses (if moved 40+ km closer to work/school)
- Employment expenses (if required by employer, Form T2200)
- Capital gains deduction (for qualifying small business shares, farm/fishing property)
- Northern residents deduction (Form T2222)

## How Federal Tax is Calculated
1. Start with total income (Line 15000)
2. Subtract deductions to get net income (Line 23600)
3. Subtract additional deductions to get taxable income (Line 26000)
4. Apply bracket rates to taxable income = gross federal tax
5. Subtract non-refundable tax credits = basic federal tax
6. Subtract dividend tax credit, foreign tax credit, etc. = net federal tax
7. Add back: tax on split income, OAS repayment, etc.

## Marginal Tax Rate Concept
The marginal tax rate is the rate on the next dollar of income. It determines the tax savings from deductions:
- A $10,000 RRSP deduction for someone in the 29% federal bracket saves $2,900 in federal tax (plus provincial savings).
- The combined marginal rate (federal + provincial) determines the total tax impact.
